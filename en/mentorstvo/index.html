About mentorship

Beyond knowing.
Understanding why.

Between knowing a technique and making a considered clinical decision, there is room for questions, reflection and experience.

Professional mentorship can help you approach this thoughtfully: connecting theory with practice, considering alternatives and learning to explain your choices. A practising clinician brings something that a fixed algorithm cannot fully capture: context, nuance and attention to the individual.

This page presents a potential mentorship direction for Zoryana Tepla. It is an invitation to discuss a professional enquiry; no programmes or training courses are currently announced.

Possible mentorship topics

The starting point:
your questions.

These topics can be a starting point for a conversation. The scope is agreed individually after discussing your enquiry.

Clinical case analysis

From initial findings to a reasoned decision: what to consider, which questions to ask and where conclusions have their limits.

Orthodontic treatment planning

The reasoning behind each step, choosing an approach and assessing its possibilities and limitations.

Diagnostic reasoning

How to connect symptoms, examination findings and investigations, and identify information that is still missing.

An interdisciplinary approach

Working with colleagues across disciplines, establishing shared goals and coordinating stages of patient care.

Professional questions

Space for specific questions from practice, discussing alternatives and finding reliable sources.

How mentorship might work

A considered conversation.
A clear next step.

An indicative sequence of steps. Format, cost and availability require separate agreement.

  1. Getting to know your goals

    Discussing your experience, professional interests and the questions you would like to explore.

  2. Agreeing on a format

    Clarifying availability, topics, meeting format, frequency and cost.

  3. Exploring questions together

    If a format is agreed, exploring the selected questions and the reasoning behind clinical decisions. Patient materials may only be used with appropriate consent and without identifying information.

  4. Conclusions and next steps

    Summarising the discussion and identifying questions for further independent study.
